Privacy Policy

Coraly  ·  Last updated: June 2026

The short version: Coraly collects no data about you. Everything stays on your device and in your Apple Health app. The only exception is a monitor photo you can explicitly choose to send, to help improve reading accuracy. Nothing is ever sent automatically.

What data the app uses

Coraly asks for access to your camera and to Apple Health. Here is exactly what happens with each:

Camera

When you take a photo of your blood pressure machine, that image is processed entirely on your device to read the numbers. The photo is not saved to your camera roll and is discarded as soon as the numbers have been extracted.

There is one exception. If Coraly misreads a number and you correct it, the app may ask whether you want to send that photo and the corrected numbers to help improve accuracy. This only happens if you explicitly tap “Send & help improve”. It is never automatic, you can choose “Don’t ask again”, and if you do not send, the photo never leaves your device.

Health data

Coraly writes your blood pressure and pulse readings to Apple Health on your device. It also reads from Apple Health to display your history and graphs within the app. This data never leaves your device or Apple Health. It is not sent to any server, database, or third party, including me.

No accounts, no tracking, no analytics

Coraly has no login, no account creation, and no user profiles. The app contains no advertising, no tracking SDKs, and no analytics tools. I have no way of knowing who you are, that you use the app, or what readings you have logged.

No third parties

Coraly does not share any data with third parties because it does not collect any data in the first place. No data is sold, rented, or disclosed.

Children

Coraly does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children. As the app collects no data at all, it is safe for use by people of any age.

Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes, the updated version will be posted at this URL with a new "last updated" date. Given the nature of the app, significant changes are unlikely.
